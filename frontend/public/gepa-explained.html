How a prompt earns
its next round.

GEPA proposes better rewrite instructions, tests them on fixed pages, and learns from the edits, scores and factual mistakes. Advancing, surviving and winning are three different decisions.

01 / Where the evidence comes from

Optimization uses validation pages. Test stays separate.

Reflection
60 pages

Sampled to explain failures and generate a new prompt. Default: 2 pages per mutation.

GEPA calls this its trainset. These pages originate from the frozen P1 validation split—not P1 training data.

Selection
30 pages

The same fixed pages compare admitted candidates and define the frontier and mean reward.

GEPA calls this its valset. It also comes from P1 validation and participates in search.

Reserved assessment
Test split

Outside the optimization loop. Needed for a separate assessment of generalization.

No test-set superiority is established by the selection dashboard.

02 / One pass through the search

Reflect → propose → compare → retain.

First, evaluate the chosen seed prompt on all 30 selection pages. A promoted prompt can be the seed; “baseline” means the starting prompt for this run.

01 / CHOOSE A PARENT

Pick useful strengths

Sample a parent from the selection-page frontier, after pruning redundant tied coverage.

02 / REFLECT

Explain the mistakes

Rewrite sampled reflection pages. Feed source, queries, edits, raw P1 and factual findings to the reflection model.

03 / PROPOSE & SCREEN

Test a new procedure

Validate the proposed prompt; allow one logged repair. Compare child and parent on the same sampled pages.

04 / FULL SELECTION

Measure its range

Only a strict reflection-reward improvement advances to all 30 selection pages. Update scores and frontier.

↶ Choose the next parent and repeat—even after a rejected candidate—until a configured limit or stop condition is reached.

Current models: GPT-5-mini rewriter and reflection; GPT-5 fidelity reviewer. P2 receives the candidate rationale plus current-page queries and original P1 feedback. Other reflection pages are diagnostic context for reflection, not evidence for rewriting a different page.

03 / The signal GEPA optimizes

Better scores, minus factual mistakes.

Page reward = mean query P1 − 0.05 × unsupported blocks − 0.02 × uncertain blocks

Try the penalty

Illustration only. Values below use the dashboard’s /100 scale.

The weights are adjustable before a run. They are exploratory trade-offs, not calibrated probabilities.

Equal weight across selection pages“Mean reward /100” is 100 × the mean of the 30 page rewards. Raw P1 is displayed separately.

04 / Three decisions, three rules

Advance is not the same as survive—or win.

Decision A

Does the child advance?

Compare mean penalized reward on the same sampled reflection pages. The child must be strictly better. Ties do not advance.

A rejection skips the expensive 30-page selection batch. The proposal still counts toward the candidate-round limit; its saved traces remain inspectable.

Decision C

Which candidate is recommended?

1. Complete coverageAll 30 selection pages must have been evaluated.
2. Better mean rewardStrictly exceed the run’s seed-prompt mean selection reward.
3. No higher execution-failure rateFactual concerns are penalized separately from execution failures.
4. Highest eligible mean winsPromotion is an explicit user action, with compatibility checks and recorded source-rejection blocks. Recommendation alone does not certify factual correctness.
Decision B

Who survives on the page-wise frontier?

A candidate gets the UI’s frontier badge if it has the best reward on at least one selection page, including ties. A lower-mean specialist can remain useful.

Actual parent sampling has an extra step: GEPA removes a candidate if every page it wins remains covered by other surviving winners. It checks lower aggregate scores first. It then samples remaining candidates in proportion to the number of pages they win. The UI badge shows page-best membership before this pruning.

This implementation uses GEPA’s instance frontier over page rewards. Raw P1 and fidelity are combined into one scalar per page; they are not separate Pareto axes. It is not an enumeration of every mathematically nondominated reward vector.

The boundaries

What makes the loop stop?

Search budget

Current defaults: 1,000 rewrite attempts or 50 candidate proposals, whichever limit stops work first.

Seed selection and reflection rewrites count. Full batches must fit the remaining attempt budget, so a run may stop below the cap. Reflection/judge/embedding calls are additional.

Operational failure

Operator Stop, technical circuit breaker, or an unrecoverable evaluation error can stop the run.

Defaults: 3 consecutive technical failures or a 20% failure threshold after at least 10 attempts. Original P1 unavailability can abort evaluation immediately.

What does not stop it

A rejected child or a factual penalty alone does not stop search. The next round can proceed.

The log’s 1,000-event display is only a viewing limit. Parallelism is 30 pages; local embedding-cache export is serialized to bound file handles.

Prompt procedures are limited to 6,000 characters by default. All LLM requests preflight complete input, schema, output reserve and overhead against a conservative 128k budget. Schema, source identity, protected-content and language failures remain hard failures. Failed/abstained proposals retain original-page scores with their statuses visible; they are not silently dropped from denominators.
